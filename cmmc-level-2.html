Where you probably are

Your SPRS score is the scoreboard. Most unprepared shops are negative.

Every requirement you miss subtracts weighted points from a maximum of 110. In our assessments, a typical small subcontractor that hasn't formally tackled 800-171 meets roughly half the requirements — and lands below zero. Certification needs 88 or better with a plan for the rest.

Straight answers

FAQ

How do I know if this applies to my shop?

Two quick checks: does DFARS 252.204-7012 appear in your contracts or POs, and do you receive technical data with distribution statements or export-control markings? If yes to either, you almost certainly handle CUI and will need Level 2. If you only handle Federal Contract Information (FCI), Level 1 self-assessment applies instead — a much lighter lift we also cover.

Is it already too late to start?

No — right now is the least painful moment this work will ever have. Remediation for a typical small shop runs 8–12 weeks after a 2–3 week assessment, then you need an assessor slot. The pause removed the deadline pressure but not the workload, so you can do this properly instead of in a scramble. When the Task Force reports and the mandate returns, C3PAO capacity will be the bottleneck — firms that remediated during the freeze will be at the front of that queue with documentation already defensible to their primes.

What does the whole thing really cost?

Industry-wide, small contractors typically spend $75k–150k in the first year all-in. Our readiness work is fixed-price (assessment $4,950; remediation from $24,950). The independent assessor's fee — commonly $30k–60k for small firms — and cloud licensing are separate and paid directly. We keep both down by shrinking your assessment boundary.

The DoW paused Phase 2. Can we stop?

No — and the pause arguably raised your risk. What was suspended on July 13, 2026 is the third-party assessment mandate that was going to phase into new CUI contracts from November 10. What was not suspended: DFARS 252.204-7012, the 110 requirements of NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2, your SPRS score, and the annual affirmation a senior executive signs. A C3PAO assessment was, in effect, protection — an accredited third party stood behind your number. Without one, an overstated score is a signed misrepresentation with your executive's name on it. Primes also set their own flowdown terms and most have not relaxed them.

Can't we just self-attest like before?

You can attest — you cannot attest inaccurately. Self-assessment has been required as a condition of award in applicable contracts since November 2025, and that requirement is unaffected by the Phase 2 pause. Submitting an SPRS score your environment doesn't support is a False Claims Act exposure: DOJ has recovered tens of millions from contractors whose scores didn't match reality. Our assessment exists to give you a number you can actually defend.

Do you certify us?

No — and be wary of anyone who says they can prep you and certify you. Certification comes only from an independent C3PAO, which is prohibited from consulting on what it assesses. We prepare you, then coordinate the assessment.

We're a 15-person shop with one IT person. Is this survivable?

Yes — that's our core client. The workload lands on us, not your IT person: we design the enclave, implement controls, write the SSP and policies, and hand your team a defined operating rhythm. Your part is decisions and access, roughly a few hours a week during the sprint.
